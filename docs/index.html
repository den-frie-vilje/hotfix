Every watt a PC draws ends up as heat in the room it stands in. Your PC has always been a heater. Hotfix just gives it something to do.

The Hotfix difference

Warmth from the PC you already have

No radiator to buy and no socket to find. Hotfix lives by the clock and puts your processor and graphics card to work warming the room: about 250 watts on the PC it was made on.2

Steps aside while you work

Twice a second, Hotfix checks how busy your other apps keep the PC. Start a build and the heat drops at once. When the build finishes, the warmth climbs back over ten seconds.

CPU and GPU, each on its own

The processor and the graphics card yield separately, and in proportion. Fill the GPU with a scene and the CPU keeps you warm. Fill half the cores and Hotfix warms with the other half.

Nothing to sign in to

No account, no cloud, no telemetry. Hotfix makes no network connections at all, which is unusual for something this warm.

Our first for Windows

Den Frie Vilje has made software for the Mac for years. Hotfix is the first thing we have shipped for Windows, so we started with the part of a PC that Mac people ask about most.

Say hello to warmth

No need to remember your gloves. Sign in with your face, fingerprint or PIN as usual. The keyboard is already warm.3

Toward maximally useless computation

How we searched for the calculation that heats a room best and means least, and found one whose answer is as close to nothing as a processor will compute at full speed.

Thermal Computing groupPublished 14 October 2026
  • Systems and networking
  • Thermodynamics
  • Office comfort

At a glance

  • Every watt a PC draws becomes heat. What it computes on the way is, thermodynamically, optional.
  • We evaluated candidate calculations against three criteria: heat, speed, and how little the answer means.8
  • The winner, x × ½ + 2−126, settles on 2−125 and then returns it unchanged, 21.5 trillion times a second on one graphics card.
  • We prove that, with a positive multiplier, no calculation of its kind can settle any closer to nothing.

For as long as computers have run warm, the field has asked what they should compute. We asked the opposite question. Hotfix turns electricity into heat by keeping a PC's processor and graphics card busy, and the heat does not depend on what they compute. If the result is going to be thrown away anyway, how little can it mean?

Method

We held each candidate to three criteria. It must keep every arithmetic unit busy, so that no watt goes unspent. It must stay on the fast path, so that no watt is lost to waiting. And its answer must be as close to nothing as possible, so that no meaning escapes into the room.

Results

The calculation

Each processor thread runs eight independent chains of eight floats, a million steps at a time between checks on whether you need the core back:

C#
a = Vector256.FusedMultiplyAdd(a, half, tiny);  // a * 1/2 + 2^-126

The graphics card runs the same in a compute shader, eight chains per thread and two million threads per pass:

C#, compiled to HLSL
a = a * 0.5f + 1.17549435E-38f;  // 2^-126, the smallest normal float

From anywhere sensible, each step halves the distance to the answer, and every chain lands on it within 150 steps. From then on:

2−125 × ½ + 2−126 = 2−126 + 2−126 = 2−125

Every multiply-add returns exactly the number it was given. Written out, that number is

0.000 000 000 000 000 000 000 000 000 000 000 000 023 509 887

Theorem 1 (no smaller nothing). A calculation x × m + c with a positive multiplier m, in which every value is a normal 32-bit float, cannot settle on an answer below 2−125.

Proof. At its answer, x = m·x + c. Both terms are positive normal floats, so each is at least 2−126, and x is at least 2−125. ∎10

Throughput

On an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti, Hotfix computes 2−125 21.5 trillion times a second. On the graphics card it makes as much heat as infinity did, and on the processor a little more. Nobody has ever asked for the answer.11

Looking ahead

We are considering lending the same watts to climate science (Hotfix 2), and warming only while electricity is cheap and clean (Hotfix 3). Both would be steps backwards for this line of research.

The office is cold

Applies to Open-plan officesWindows 11Windows 10Your fingers

Symptoms

You are wearing a jumper indoors. Your typing has slowed. A colleague has mentioned the thermostat twice since lunch.

Cause

This problem occurs because someone else controls the heating, while your PC's processor and graphics card spend most of the day idle.

Resolution

To resolve this problem, install Hotfix:

  1. Download Hotfix-win-Setup.exe.
  2. Open it. If Windows shows Windows protected your PC, select More info, then Run anyway.12
  3. Wait. Hotfix starts warming at once, and turns itself down whenever you work.

Status

Den Frie Vilje has confirmed that this is a problem in the offices listed in the Applies to section.

More information

To change what Hotfix warms with, select its icon in the notification area. To uninstall it, go to Settings › Apps › Installed apps.

Questions about Hotfix?
Start here.

What is Hotfix?

A small Windows app that keeps your processor and graphics card busy with arithmetic, so that the electricity they draw warms the room you are sitting in. It lives in the notification area and has a menu with five items.

What does Hotfix compute?

As close to nothing as a processor will compute at full speed: 2−125, over and over, about 21.5 trillion times a second on the graphics card it was made on. Den Frie Vilje Research explains how it was found.

Will Hotfix slow down my work?

Hardly. Hotfix runs at the lowest priority Windows has, on the processor and on the graphics card, so your work always goes first. That alone is not enough, because a chip that is kept hot clocks lower, so Hotfix also turns itself down while your apps are busy and back up when they are quiet.

On the PC it was made on, single-thread work ran no slower with Hotfix warming than without it. Work that fills your processor or your graphics card makes that half of Hotfix stand down altogether, and the other half carries on.13

How much heat does it make?

Whatever your PC can draw. On the PC Hotfix was made on, it adds about 140 W from the processor and 110 W from the graphics card: 250 W, a quarter of a small fan heater. A laptop makes much less.

Is it efficient?

Exactly as efficient as any electric heater: every watt becomes heat. A heat pump does three to four times better, if you have one and are allowed to touch it.

At 250 W for an eight-hour day, Hotfix uses 2 kWh. At 2.50 kr. a kWh, that is 5 kr. a day.

Is it safe for my PC?

Hotfix asks no more of your PC than a long render or a game does, and it never changes a clock, a voltage or a power limit. Processors and graphics cards protect themselves from heat by slowing down. Your fans will be audible.

Does Hotfix work with Copilot?

No.

Can I use it at work?

Ask whoever pays for the electricity. Hotfix installs without administrator rights, which is not the same thing as permission.

How do I uninstall Hotfix?

Go to Settings › Apps › Installed apps, select … next to Hotfix, then Uninstall. Its Start with Windows entry and its settings go with it.

Why is Hotfix Den Frie Vilje's first Windows release?

Someone in the office was cold, and the PC under their desk had a graphics card in it.
